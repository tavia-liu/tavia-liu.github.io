Why It Matters

My Global Studies major is not separate from my AI work. It changes the questions I ask.

Computer science trains me to build models and systems. Global Studies trains me to notice power: whose language is treated as standard, whose records are searchable, whose movement is criminalized, whose land is made available to extraction, and whose knowledge gets translated into policy.

May 2024 / Cuetzalan, Mexico

Community Resistance, Migration, and Indigenous Sovereignty

Location: Cuetzalan, Puebla, Mexico. Academic partner: Augsburg University. This May 2024 field study examined global citizenship from subaltern perspectives, with sustained attention to systemic violence, Indigenous sovereignty, migration, territorial defense, and community-led alternatives to extractive development.

We met with migrants, social movement leaders, Indigenous youth organizers, community elders, cooperative members, and local organizers confronting the lived effects of US immigration policy, extractive industries, neoliberal development, and the long legacy of colonization in Mexico and Latin America.

One major thread was territorial defense in Masejwual territory: how cooperative models and community institutions resist mega-projects such as mining and hydroelectric dams while sustaining life-affirming social practices. The experience pushed me to think about security beyond technical systems: security as land, memory, language, kinship, water, and the capacity of communities to govern their futures.

June-July 2025 / Roma, Italy

Institutions, Development Policy, and International Relations

Location: Roma, Italy. Academic supervision: Francesca Astengo, Université de Montréal. This June-July 2025 field study focused on international institutions, development policy, migration, Europe-Gulf relations, and the translation of research into policy practice.

In Roma, I visited and discussed international affairs with scholars and policy experts at the Euro-Gulf Information Centre and CeSPI. At CeSPI, the conversation with Marco Zupi connected development cooperation, evaluation, migration, and policy research to the practical work of institutional decision-making.

The Italy experience gave me a clearer view of how think tanks, research institutes, and international-policy communities produce knowledge for governments, civil society, and transnational publics. It also sharpened my interest in building AI tools that are useful in politically complex domains without flattening local histories or human stakes.
